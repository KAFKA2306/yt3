# Yawa recurring episode experience

Use this skill to review or improve a Yawa Archive episode concept, script, or recurring production format. Optimize for the listener's experience and the episode's reason to be heard through, not for joke count. Curiosity, sensory discovery, companionship, a small surprise, recognition, and a satisfying afterglow can all make an episode enjoyable.

The installed `sakurai` skill is a game-design reference, not a comedy rubric. When its broader principles help, use that skill and consult only relevant sections on planning, gameplay, mindset, or sound. Translate those principles to an audio story; do not transfer game mechanics literally. The source is an unofficial AI summary of a YouTube series, so use it as a design lens rather than as an authoritative quotation. Source: https://github.com/kociden/sakurai-skill

## Review an episode

1. Read the concept or script and any recent episodes supplied for comparison. Use the repository's current Yawa instructions and constraints as authoritative.
2. State the episode's unique listener promise in one sentence. Check whether the opening reaches that experience promptly.
3. Identify where the listener gets a concrete sensory detail, discovery, emotional change, or other small reward. Check that the episode progresses and that its ending gives a fitting resolution or afterglow.
4. Check whether the idea is distinct from recent episodes. Vary the situation, listener role, sensory focus, relationship, emotional turn, or ending when repetition weakens the experience.
5. Preserve close, quiet ASMR pacing. Use mild tension only when the episode can resolve it naturally. Do not require jokes, twists, constant stimulation, or a fixed arc.

When the user requests a score, rate these dimensions from 1 to 5 and cite evidence from the supplied scene or line: distinct listener promise, early appeal, progression, payoff, ASMR fit, and difference from recent episodes. A 1 means absent or disruptive, 3 means present but uneven or familiar, and 5 means clear and effective. Treat the ratings as advisory judgments, not measurements or production gates.

When asked to improve a draft, name the single change with the most benefit, then revise only what is needed. Do not add model calls, automated retries, or publication gates unless the user explicitly asks for those changes.
